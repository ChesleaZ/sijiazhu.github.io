Research project

Scaling Laws in Single-Cell Representation Learning

Research Assistant, Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health

Advisors: Prof. Rong Ma and Prof. Allon Klein

  • Single-cell multi-omics
  • Random Matrix Theory
  • Representation learning
  • Foundation models

Motivation

Single-cell foundation models are trained on ever-larger collections of cells, but it is still unclear how much biological information their representations actually recover, and how that depends on the amount and quality of the data. Understanding these scaling behaviors helps decide where additional data — more cells, deeper sequencing, or cleaner batches — is most valuable.

Approach

  • Applying Random Matrix Theory to derive mutual-information-based measures of biological signal recovery in single-cell multi-omics data.
  • Developing theoretical scaling laws for cell number, sequencing depth, and batch effects.
  • Evaluating how representations from models such as scVI, Geneformer, and scGPT recover biological information on real data.
  • Connecting cell representations to language models through cell-to-text adapters, so that what the representation captures can be interpreted in natural language.

Experimental design

A further focus is experimental design under fixed budgets: how to balance cell number and sequencing depth when collecting single-cell data. Applications include systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) and Perturb-seq datasets.

Related publication
