Mathematical Foundations of Machine Learning under Uncertainty

Undergraduate research, 2023–2025.

Many learning problems have to work with information that is vague or only partly known: a patient's symptoms only partly match a disease, or a pixel sits between two regions of an image. Fuzzy sets and their extensions describe each element by how strongly it belongs, does not belong, or is undecided. During my undergraduate studies I worked on the basic tools needed to learn from this kind of data: ways to measure how different two uncertain objects are, and clustering methods built on top of those measures.

Distance and divergence measures

Picture fuzzy sets, Fermatean fuzzy sets and their complex-valued versions each describe uncertainty in a slightly different way, and the usual distances do not always behave sensibly on them. I proposed new distance measures and α-divergence measures for these settings, checked that they satisfy the properties a distance or divergence should have, and compared them with existing measures on examples where the older ones give counter-intuitive answers. One of these papers gives a single parametric divergence operator that covers several earlier measures as special cases.

Clustering

The same question, how far apart two objects are, sits at the centre of clustering. I worked on c-means type algorithms that learn a weight for each feature automatically, so that uninformative features count less, and applied them to colour image segmentation.

Applications

I tested these methods on pattern recognition, medical diagnosis, multi-criteria decision making and image segmentation problems.

Looking back

This work taught me to think carefully about what a measure of similarity or information should satisfy before using it. That question carried over into my current research, where I study how much biological signal a learned representation actually recovers.
