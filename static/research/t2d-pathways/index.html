Research project

Pathway-Independent Genetic Signals in Type 2 Diabetes Clusters

Research Assistant, Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard

Advisors: Prof. Kenneth Westerman and Prof. Miriam Udler

  • Type 2 diabetes
  • Polygenic scores
  • Genetic clustering
  • Pathway analysis

Motivation

Data-driven genetic clustering has been used to identify distinct mechanisms and subtypes of type 2 diabetes (T2D). However, cluster definitions depend on the study population, phenotype inputs and clustering method, and interpreting a cluster requires assigning biological meaning to a group of variants. Annotation-based pathway polygenic scores (pPGS) offer a complementary view: within a fixed pathway database, gene-to-pathway mappings give consistent features that can be compared across studies, tissues and diseases.

Approach

  • Calculating cluster polygenic scores from published T2D genetic cluster definitions (Kim et al., Smith et al., Suzuki et al.) in the All of Us Research Program.
  • Constructing tissue-specific Reactome pathway polygenic scores from GTEx v8 gene-expression prediction models, and reconstructing each cluster score as a weighted combination of pathway scores.
  • Evaluating how stable cluster scores and assignments are across studies and when the phenotype inputs used for clustering change.
  • Testing pathway–tissue associations with the original cluster scores and with genetic factor analysis (GFA)–derived scores.

Clinical relevance

The project also asks whether pathway-based scores carry clinically relevant information: whether pathway-predicted lipodystrophy scores preserve the ancestry differences in the BMI–T2D relationship, whether pathway scores improve prediction of incident retinopathy and neuropathy, and whether the same pathway-derived scores are relevant to coronary artery disease.

Project status

Ongoing work. Results are preliminary and have not yet been peer reviewed.
